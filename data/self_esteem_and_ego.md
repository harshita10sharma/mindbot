# Self-esteem, ego and self-compassion

Healthy self-esteem means valuing yourself while seeing yourself realistically, including strengths and weaknesses. Fragile self-esteem, on the other hand, often needs constant validation and feels threatened by criticism. It can look like arrogance, defensiveness, or always needing to be right, but underneath there is often insecurity.

Signs of fragile self-worth: difficulty accepting feedback, needing to win every argument, constantly comparing yourself to others, blaming others for setbacks, or swinging between feeling superior and feeling worthless.

Self-compassion (Kristin Neff) is a research-backed alternative to chasing high self-esteem. It has three parts:
1. Self-kindness: speak to yourself as you would to a good friend.
2. Common humanity: mistakes and struggles are part of being human, not proof that you are uniquely flawed.
3. Mindfulness: notice painful feelings without exaggerating or suppressing them.

Research suggests people with more self-compassion are more willing to take responsibility for their mistakes, because admitting them feels less threatening. Security, not superiority, is what lets us grow.

Try this: think of a recent criticism that stung. Ask, "Is there any part of this that is useful to me?" and "What would I say to a friend who received this feedback?"
