# Behavioural activation: small steps for low mood

When mood is low, people naturally do less: they withdraw, stay in bed, and stop doing things they used to enjoy. Doing less then makes mood even lower. Behavioural activation breaks this cycle by scheduling small, meaningful activities without waiting to "feel like it" first. Motivation often follows action, not the other way round.

How to use it:
1. List activities that used to give you a sense of pleasure (like music or a walk) or achievement (like tidying one shelf).
2. Pick one very small activity for today. Make it specific: what, when, where. For example, "a 10-minute walk after lunch".
3. Do it, then rate your mood before and after from 0 to 10.
4. Gradually build up, and include activities linked to your values, like family, learning, health or kindness.

Behavioural activation is an evidence-based treatment for depression, and research has found it can be about as effective as full cognitive therapy for many people.
