# Grounding and breathing for panic and overwhelm

When anxiety or panic spikes, the body's alarm system (fight-or-flight) is switched on. Grounding and slow breathing help signal safety to the nervous system.

Paced breathing: breathe in through the nose for 4 counts, hold for 2, and breathe out slowly through the mouth for 6 counts. A longer out-breath than in-breath activates the body's calming response. Repeat for 1 to 3 minutes.

Box breathing: in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4. Repeat 4 times.

5-4-3-2-1 grounding: name 5 things you can see, 4 things you can feel, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste. This pulls attention out of anxious thoughts and into the present moment.

Temperature: holding something cold, or splashing cold water on your face, can quickly reduce intense emotion. This is one of the DBT "TIPP" skills.

About panic attacks: they feel frightening (racing heart, breathlessness, dizziness, fear of losing control) but they usually peak within about 10 minutes and pass. They are not dangerous in themselves. If you have chest pain or symptoms you are unsure about, seek medical care to rule out physical causes.
