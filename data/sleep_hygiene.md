# Sleep and mental health

Sleep and mood affect each other. Poor sleep worsens anxiety, low mood and concentration, and stress makes sleep harder.

Habits that help (sleep hygiene):
1. Keep a regular wake-up time every day, including weekends.
2. Use the bed only for sleep. If you can't sleep after about 20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light, and return when sleepy.
3. Avoid caffeine after early afternoon, and avoid heavy meals and alcohol close to bedtime.
4. Reduce screens and bright light in the hour before bed.
5. Get daylight in the morning and some physical activity during the day.
6. Write down worries and a to-do list before bed to "park" them.
7. Keep daytime naps short, under about 30 minutes.

Persistent insomnia lasting more than a few weeks is worth discussing with a doctor. CBT for Insomnia (CBT-I) is the recommended first-line treatment and works better long-term than sleeping pills.
