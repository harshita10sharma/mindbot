# When and where to seek professional help

Consider professional help if:
- Low mood, anxiety or stress has lasted more than two weeks and affects sleep, study, work or relationships.
- You have thoughts of death, suicide or self-harm (seek help immediately).
- You are using alcohol or drugs to cope.
- You have experienced trauma that keeps intruding on daily life.
- You hear or see things others don't, or feel that people are plotting against you.

Who does what:
- Counsellors offer talking support for life stresses, often in schools, colleges and workplaces.
- Clinical psychologists assess and provide structured therapies such as CBT. In India, they are registered with the Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI).
- Psychiatrists are medical doctors who diagnose mental-health conditions, prescribe and manage medication, and rule out physical causes.
- General physicians are often a good first step, and can refer you onward.

In India, Tele-MANAS (call 14416 or 1800-891-4416) is a free, 24x7 government mental-health helpline that can connect you to counsellors and, when needed, to specialists.

About medication: never start, stop or change psychiatric medication (such as antidepressants, anti-anxiety medicines or sleeping pills) without talking to your doctor or psychiatrist. Stopping suddenly can cause withdrawal effects or relapse.

An AI companion like MindBot can support you between sessions, but it cannot diagnose, prescribe, or replace a professional.
